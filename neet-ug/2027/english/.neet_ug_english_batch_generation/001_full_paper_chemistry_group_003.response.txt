  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>In qualitative inorganic analysis, group IV cations such as \(\text{Ni}^{2+}\) are confirmed using specific chelating reagents that form distinctively coloured insoluble coordination complexes.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>Potassium ferrocyanide is used to confirm \(\text{Fe}^{3+}\) (Prussian blue) or \(\text{Cu}^{2+}\) (chocolate-brown). Ammonium molybdate is used for phosphate detection, and Nessler's reagent confirms \(\text{NH}_4^+\) ions (brown precipitate).</p><p><strong>Step 3: Application</strong><br/>In an ammoniacal medium, \(\text{Ni}^{2+}\) reacts with dimethylglyoxime (DMG) to form a square-planar bis(dimethylglyoximato)nickel(II) complex, which appears as a characteristic bright rosy-red precipitate.</p><p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The reagent used to confirm \(\text{Ni}^{2+}\) ions is dimethylglyoxime in ammoniacal medium.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>What volume (in \(\text{mL}\)) of \(0.02\text{ M}\) \(\text{KMnO}_4\) solution is required to completely oxidize \(20\text{ mL}\) of \(0.1\text{ M}\) Mohr's salt (\(\text{FeSO}_4\cdot(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4\cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}\)) solution in dilute \(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\) medium?</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Molarity of \(\text{KMnO}_4\) solution, \(M_1 = 0.02\text{ M}\). Volume of Mohr's salt solution, \(V_2 = 20\text{ mL}\). Molarity of Mohr's salt solution, \(M_2 = 0.1\text{ M}\). Medium: dilute \(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\).</p><p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>In Mohr's salt, only \(\text{Fe}^{2+}\) ions undergo oxidation to \(\text{Fe}^{3+}\) by \(\text{MnO}_4^-\) in acidic medium, while \(\text{MnO}_4^-\) is reduced to \(\text{Mn}^{2+}\).</p><p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>Balanced ionic equation: \[\text{MnO}_4^- + 5\text{Fe}^{2+} + 8\text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{Fe}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}\] Thus, the \(n\)-factor for \(\text{KMnO}_4\) is \(n_1 = 5\) (oxidation state of \(\text{Mn}\) changes from \(+7\) to \(+2\)), and the \(n\)-factor for Mohr's salt is \(n_2 = 1\) (oxidation state of \(\text{Fe}\) changes from \(+2\) to \(+3\)). By the law of chemical equivalence: \[n_1 M_1 V_1 = n_2 M_2 V_2\]</p><p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>Substitute the given values into the equivalence equation: \[5 \times 0.02\text{ M} \times V_1 = 1 \times 0.1\text{ M} \times 20\text{ mL}\] \[0.10 \times V_1 = 2.0\text{ mmol}\] \[V_1 = \frac{2.0}{0.10} = 20\text{ mL}\]</p><p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>Check by moles: millimoles of \(\text{Fe}^{2+} = 0.1 \times 20 = 2.0\text{ mmol}\). Since \(1\text{ mol}\) \(\text{MnO}_4^-\) oxidizes \(5\text{ mol}\) \(\text{Fe}^{2+}\), millimoles of \(\text{KMnO}_4\) needed \(= \frac{2.0}{5} = 0.4\text{ mmol}\). Volume \(= \frac{0.4\text{ mmol}}{0.02\text{ M}} = 20\text{ mL}\). The required volume of \(\text{KMnO}_4\) solution is \(20\text{ mL}\).</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>
