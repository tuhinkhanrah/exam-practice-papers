  <generalfeedback format="html"><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>We are given three fundamental physical constants: Planck's constant \(h\), the speed of light in vacuum \(c\), and the universal gravitational constant \(G\). We need to find the dimensions of the composite expression \(\sqrt{\frac{h c}{G}}\) and identify the physical quantity it represents.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>We use the standard SI fundamental dimensions: mass \([\mathrm{M}]\), length \([\mathrm{L}]\), and time \([\mathrm{T}]\). All numerical constants and exponents are treated algebraically.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>From \(E = h\nu\), the dimensions of Planck's constant are \([h] = \frac{[E]}{[\nu]} = \frac{[\mathrm{M}\mathrm{L}^2\mathrm{T}^{-2}]}{[\mathrm{T}^{-1}]} = [\mathrm{M}\mathrm{L}^2\mathrm{T}^{-1}]\).<br/>The dimensions of speed of light are \([c] = [\mathrm{L}\mathrm{T}^{-1}]\).<br/>From Newton's law of gravitation \(F = \frac{G m_1 m_2}{r^2}\), the dimensions of \(G\) are \([G] = \frac{[F][r]^2}{[m]^2} = \frac{[\mathrm{M}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{T}^{-2}][\mathrm{L}^2]}{[\mathrm{M}^2]} = [\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{L}^3\mathrm{T}^{-2}]\).</p><p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>Substitute the dimensional formulae into \(\frac{h c}{G}\):<br/>\[\left[\frac{h c}{G}\right] = \frac{[\mathrm{M}\mathrm{L}^2\mathrm{T}^{-1}][\mathrm{L}\mathrm{T}^{-1}]}{[\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{L}^3\mathrm{T}^{-2}]} = \frac{[\mathrm{M}\mathrm{L}^3\mathrm{T}^{-2}]}{[\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{L}^3\mathrm{T}^{-2}]} = [\mathrm{M}^2]\]<br/>Taking the square root of both sides:<br/>\[\left[\sqrt{\frac{h c}{G}}\right] = \left([\mathrm{M}^2]\right)^{1/2} = [\mathrm{M}]\]</p><p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>The resulting dimension \([\mathrm{M}]\) has no length or time dependence and corresponds to mass (specifically, the Planck mass).
